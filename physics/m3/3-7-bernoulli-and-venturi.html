3.7  Bernoulli, Venturi, and the Coandă Effect

Conservation of energy applied to a moving fluid, and the three devices in every operating room that exploit it.

On finishing this section you should be able to
  • State Bernoulli's principle as an energy conservation statement.
  • Explain entrainment via the Venturi effect and compute a fixed FiO2 device's behaviour.
  • Describe the Coandă effect and its clinical consequences.
  • Apply the modified Bernoulli equation to echocardiographic pressure gradients.

Bernoulli as energy conservation

For an ideal fluid in steady flow, the total energy per unit volume is constant along a streamline. That energy has three forms, and they trade against one another.

P  +  ½ρv²  +  ρgh  =  constant P = static pressure · ½ρv² = kinetic energy per unit volume, the dynamic pressure · ρgh = potential energy per unit volume
What you already have

This is conservation of mechanical energy with each term divided by volume. If you have done any fluid mechanics, the only new material here is which clinical device exploits which term.

The consequence that matters: at constant height, where velocity rises, static pressure falls. Fluid speeding through a constriction has converted pressure energy into kinetic energy.

Venturi and entrainment

Gas flows through a constriction. Continuity requires velocity to rise as area falls.
By Bernoulli, static pressure at the constriction drops below ambient.
A side port at that point sees a negative pressure relative to the room.
Room air is drawn in, or entrained, through the port.
The entrainment ratio is fixed by the geometry, so the delivered mixture is predictable.
Clinical anchor

A Venturi mask delivers a fixed FIO2 precisely because the entrainment ratio is geometric. A 24% valve entrains about 25 litres of air per litre of oxygen; a 60% valve entrains about 1 litre per litre. Because total delivered flow exceeds the patient's peak inspiratory flow, dilution by room air does not occur and the FIO2 holds regardless of respiratory pattern. That is the entire reason it is used in patients where a precise, modest oxygen concentration matters.

The same principle drives jet nebulisers, Venturi suction devices, and jet ventilation, where a high-velocity oxygen jet entrains additional gas to augment tidal volume.

The modified Bernoulli equation

In echocardiography, dropping the negligible terms and substituting blood density gives a usable clinical shortcut.

ΔP  ≈  4v² ΔP in mmHg, v = peak velocity across the lesion in m/s. Estimating pulmonary artery systolic pressure from a tricuspid regurgitant jet is the everyday application.

A TR jet velocity of 3.5 m/s implies a right ventricle to right atrium gradient of 4 × 12.25 ≈ 49 mmHg. Add an estimated right atrial pressure and you have a non-invasive PASP.

The Coandă effect

A fluid jet tends to adhere to a nearby curved surface rather than travelling in a straight line, because entrainment on the surface side is restricted and the resulting pressure difference bends the jet toward the wall.

SettingConsequence
Airway bifurcationsGas preferentially follows one branch, producing uneven distribution and contributing to asymmetric mucus plugging
Vascular bifurcationsUneven flow division, relevant to atheroma distribution and to eccentric regurgitant jets
EchocardiographyA wall-hugging regurgitant jet appears smaller than it is, so eccentric jets are systematically underestimated in severity
Fluidic ventilatorsDeliberately exploited for valveless flow switching
Why does a Venturi mask deliver a reliable FiO₂ when a simple face mask does not?

Total flow. The Venturi device generates a delivered flow that exceeds the patient's peak inspiratory flow, so the patient never has to draw supplementary room air around the mask. A simple mask delivers less than peak inspiratory flow, so entrained room air dilutes it by a variable amount depending on respiratory pattern, and the actual FIO2 becomes unpredictable.

An eccentric mitral regurgitant jet hugging the atrial wall is reported as mild. Why be sceptical?

The Coandă effect. A wall-adherent jet spreads less and produces a smaller colour Doppler area than a central jet of identical regurgitant volume, so area-based grading systematically underestimates eccentric jets. Volumetric or PISA methods are less vulnerable.
