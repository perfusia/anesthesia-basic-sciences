Module 3 Exam

All eight sections plus the lab. Calculation-weighted, with the regime distinction carrying the most items.

Exam conditions
  • First answer locks. No exceptions.
  • For any flow question, identify the regime before choosing an equation.
  • Radius is fourth power in laminar flow and fifth power in turbulent flow.
  • Copy your misses at the end and paste them into chat.
Before you start

First answer is final. Change an answer only if you can name a concrete error: misread the stem, clicked the wrong option. Never because of a feeling.

The three traps this module is built to catch: viscosity governs laminar flow while density governs turbulent flow; a wide tube favours turbulence at fixed velocity, and a stenosis causes it by raising velocity instead; and Poiseuille describes a segment, not a circuit.

Module 3 Exam · 24 items

First answer locks · mechanism appears on lock
Q1
Flow in a tube varies with the fourth power of radius because:
MechanismArea gives one r², and mean velocity rising with r² at fixed gradient gives the second. Their product produces the fourth power.
Q2
Which is the most effective access for rapid volume resuscitation?
MechanismShort and wide wins on both the r⁴ and the 1/L terms. Central catheters are long with narrow individual lumens and lose on both.
Q3
Mean velocity relative to peak velocity in laminar flow is:
MechanismIntegrating the parabolic profile over the cross-section gives exactly half. In turbulent flow the profile flattens and mean approaches peak.
Q4
Reynolds number above 4000 indicates:
MechanismBelow 2000 is laminar, 2000 to 4000 transitional, above 4000 fully turbulent. Poiseuille applies only in the laminar regime.
Q5
In turbulent flow, the governing fluid property is:
MechanismInertial effects dominate above the transition, so density governs. Viscosity is the governing property only in laminar flow.
Q6
Heliox reduces resistance in large-airway obstruction because helium:
MechanismLower density lowers Reynolds number, which can restore laminar flow. Helium's viscosity is slightly higher than nitrogen's, not lower.
Q7
Heliox is least effective in:
MechanismDistal airway flow is already laminar owing to low velocity and enormous total cross-sectional area, so viscosity governs and a density change achieves little.
Q8
The maximum FiO2 achievable with an 80:20 heliox mixture is:
MechanismThe mixture is 20% oxygen by definition. Below roughly 60% helium the density advantage disappears, so heliox and high FiO2 are mutually exclusive.
Q9
Resistances in parallel combine such that total resistance is:
MechanismReciprocals add, so each additional path increases total conductance and lowers total resistance below any single element.
Q10
Small airways contribute only 10 to 20% of total airway resistance because they are:
MechanismBranching multiplies parallel paths faster than radius falls, so total cross-sectional area rises enormously and reciprocal combination drives resistance down.
Q11
As velocity rises through a constriction, static pressure:
MechanismBernoulli conserves total energy along a streamline, so a rise in the kinetic term must be paid for from the static pressure term.
Q12
A Venturi mask delivers a predictable FiO2 because:
MechanismWith total delivered flow exceeding demand, no variable dilution by room air occurs, so the fixed geometric entrainment ratio sets the concentration.
Q13
A tricuspid regurgitant jet velocity of 4.0 m/s implies a gradient of:
MechanismModified Bernoulli gives ΔP ≈ 4v² = 4 × 16 = 64 mmHg. Adding estimated right atrial pressure yields pulmonary artery systolic pressure.
Q14
The Coandă effect causes a fluid jet to:
MechanismRestricted entrainment on the surface side produces a pressure difference that bends the jet toward the wall. Entrainment through a port is the separate Venturi effect.
Q15
Blood viscosity in a low-flow state:
MechanismBlood is shear-thinning, so reduced shear allows rouleaux to form and viscosity to rise, increasing resistance exactly when perfusion is already inadequate.
Q16
The Fahraeus-Lindqvist effect describes:
MechanismErythrocytes migrate axially, leaving a low-viscosity plasma layer at the wall that lubricates the high-shear region. It reverses below about 5 to 7 μm.
Q17
The dominant determinant of whole blood viscosity is:
MechanismHaematocrit outweighs all other contributors and the relationship steepens markedly at high values, which is the rationale for deliberate haemodilution on bypass.
Q18
Doubling infusion tubing length changes flow by a factor of:
MechanismLength is inverse linear in Poiseuille, so doubling it halves flow. Fourth-power behaviour belongs to radius alone.
Q19
Turbulent flow carries a fifth-power dependence on radius.
MechanismFrom Darcy-Weisbach, turbulent flow varies with r⁵ rather than the laminar r⁴. Turbulence makes geometry matter more, not less.
Q20
Laminar flow is silent because it produces no pressure fluctuation.
MechanismSound requires fluctuating pressure. Steady layered flow generates none, whereas turbulent eddies produce the broadband noise heard as stridor or a bruit.
Q21
Doubling one arteriole's radius produces a sixteenfold rise in cardiac output.
MechanismPoiseuille describes flow through a segment at a fixed gradient across it. Once that segment stops limiting, its pressure drop collapses and the constraint moves elsewhere in the circuit.
Q22
Write the Reynolds number equation.
MechanismRe = ρvD/η, the ratio of inertial to viscous forces. It is dimensionless, which is why one threshold applies across every geometry.
Q23
MAP 95, CVP 15, cardiac output 5 L/min. Give SVR in dyn·s·cm⁻⁵.
Mechanism[(95 − 15)/5] × 80 = 16 × 80 = 1280. The CVP subtraction is the step most often skipped.
Q24
Name the two governing fluid properties, for laminar then turbulent flow, in that order.
MechanismViscosity governs laminar flow through the η term in Poiseuille. Density governs turbulent flow through the inertial term. Reversing them inverts every prediction about heliox.
