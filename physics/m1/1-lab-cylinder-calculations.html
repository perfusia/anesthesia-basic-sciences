Lab 1  ·  Cylinder Calculations

Worked problem set. Do these on paper first, then check. The exam questions in this module are calculation questions.

Lab objectives
  • Compute remaining contents and time to empty for an oxygen cylinder.
  • Compute nitrous oxide contents from cylinder mass.
  • Apply Gay-Lussac to a temperature-shifted cylinder using absolute temperature.
  • Compute inspired oxygen partial pressure at altitude with the humidification correction.
Reference data

Oxygen E-cylinder: 660 L at 2000 psi · Nitrous E-cylinder: ~1590 L at 745 psi, tare weight stamped on shoulder · Molar mass N2O = 44 g/mol · Molar volume = 22.4 L/mol · Saturated water vapour pressure at 37 °C = 47 mmHg · K = °C + 273

Problem 1

An oxygen E-cylinder gauge reads 900 psi. You are transporting a ventilated patient at a fresh gas flow of 8 L/min. How long until the cylinder is empty, and what would you actually plan for?

Work it

(900 / 2000) × 660 = 297 L. 297 / 8 = 37 minutes.

Plan for substantially less. Cylinder output becomes unreliable as pressure approaches the regulator's minimum, and no transport should be started on a margin this thin. The practical answer is to swap the cylinder.

Problem 2

A nitrous oxide cylinder weighs 9.2 kg. The tare weight stamped on the shoulder is 5.8 kg. How much gas will it deliver?

Work it

Contents = 9.2 − 5.8 = 3.4 kg = 3400 g.

Moles = 3400 / 44 = 77.3 mol.

Volume = 77.3 × 22.4 = 1731 L.

Problem 3

The same nitrous cylinder's gauge reads 745 psi. What does that tell you about remaining contents?

Work it

Nothing. That is the saturated vapour pressure of nitrous oxide at room temperature, and it will read 745 psi whether the cylinder is 95% full or 5% full. The gauge only begins to fall once the last liquid has evaporated. This is the entire clinical point of section 1.2.

Problem 4

An oxygen cylinder reads 1900 psi in a 19 °C storage room. It is moved to an ambulance bay at 38 °C and left in the sun. What does the gauge now read?

Work it

Convert to kelvin first: 292 K → 311 K.

P2 = 1900 × (311 / 292) = 2024 psi.

No gas was added. The rise is entirely Gay-Lussac. Running this in Celsius gives 1900 × 38/19 = 3800 psi, which is nonsense and is exactly the error the question is designed to catch.

Problem 5

A patient is anesthetised in a facility at 2,400 m where barometric pressure is 565 mmHg. You set FIO2 to 0.35. What is the inspired oxygen partial pressure, and how does it compare to the same setting at sea level?

Work it

At altitude: 0.35 × (565 − 47) = 0.35 × 518 = 181 mmHg.

At sea level: 0.35 × (760 − 47) = 0.35 × 713 = 250 mmHg.

A 28% reduction in driving pressure from an identical dial setting. Note also that the fixed 47 mmHg water vapour term consumes 8.3% of ambient pressure at altitude against 6.2% at sea level, so it bites proportionally harder as you climb.

Problem 6

You have an oxygen E-cylinder at 2000 psi and a nitrous E-cylinder at 745 psi. Running 2 L/min of each, which empties first?

Work it

Oxygen: 660 / 2 = 330 minutes.

Nitrous: 1590 / 2 = 795 minutes.

Oxygen empties first, and by a wide margin, despite reading nearly three times the pressure. The lesson is that gauge pressure and gas quantity are only linked for a true gas above its critical temperature.

Q1
An oxygen E-cylinder reads 500 psi and flow is 10 L/min. Time to empty is closest to:
Mechanism(500/2000) × 660 = 165 L. 165 / 10 = 16.5 minutes. Contents fall linearly with gauge pressure for a true gas.
Q2
A cylinder reads 2000 psi at 22 °C. Moved to 45 °C, the new reading is approximately:
MechanismKelvin conversion is mandatory: 295 K → 318 K. 2000 × 318/295 = 2156 psi. Working in Celsius produces the 4090 distractor.
Q3
Running 3 L/min from a full nitrous E-cylinder, time to empty is approximately:
Mechanism1590 L / 3 L/min = 530 minutes. The nitrous cylinder holds far more gas than its lower gauge pressure suggests because it is stored as a liquid.
Q4
A nitrous cylinder holds 2.2 kg of contents. Deliverable volume in litres, to the nearest ten.
Mechanism(2200 / 44) × 22.4 = 50 mol × 22.4 = 1120 L. Mass is the only route to contents because the gauge is pinned to vapour pressure.
Q5
Inspired oxygen partial pressure on 0.5 FiO2 at a barometric pressure of 620 mmHg, in mmHg.
Mechanism0.5 × (620 − 47) = 286.5 mmHg. The 47 mmHg humidification term is subtracted before applying the fraction, and it is pressure-independent.
