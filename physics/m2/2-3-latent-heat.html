2.3  Latent Heat of Vaporisation and Specific Heat

Why a vaporiser is a heavy lump of metal, and what would happen if it were not.

On finishing this section you should be able to
  • Define latent heat of vaporisation and specific heat capacity.
  • Explain the cooling that accompanies vaporisation and its effect on agent output.
  • Justify the material choice and mass of a variable bypass vaporiser.
  • Describe the role of the bimetallic strip in temperature compensation.

Two quantities

Latent heat of vaporisation is the energy required to convert a given mass of liquid into vapour without any change in temperature. It is spent breaking intermolecular bonds rather than raising kinetic energy, which is why the temperature does not move while it is being paid.

Specific heat capacity is the energy required to raise the temperature of one kilogram of a substance by one kelvin. High specific heat means a substance resists temperature change.

What you already have

Both quantities and the phase-change plateau on a heating curve. The new content is entirely in the engineering consequence: this is the section that explains a design decision, not a physical principle you have not met.

The problem

Fresh gas passes over liquid agent and carries vapour away.
Producing that vapour costs latent heat, which has to come from somewhere.
It comes from the remaining liquid and from the vaporiser body.
The liquid cools.
Saturated vapour pressure falls, because SVP depends on temperature.
Agent output falls, progressively, exactly when a long case needs it to be stable.

Three engineering answers

ProblemSolutionMechanism
Liquid cools during useHigh specific heat bodyStores thermal energy, resists temperature change
Heat must reach the liquidHigh thermal conductivity metalCopper and bronze move heat from the body to the sump quickly
Residual temperature driftBimetallic strip valveTwo metals with different expansion coefficients bend with temperature and mechanically alter the splitting ratio

The bimetallic strip is the elegant part. As the vaporiser cools and SVP falls, the strip bends and opens the vaporising chamber further, sending a larger fraction of carrier gas over the agent. More flow across a weaker vapour pressure yields the same output concentration. It is a purely mechanical negative feedback loop with no electronics and no power supply.

Clinical anchor

A modern vaporiser weighs several kilograms and most of that mass is thermal ballast, not structure. That weight is the reason output stays within specification at high fresh gas flows over a long case. It is also why a vaporiser that has just been refilled with cold agent, or one run hard immediately after removal from a cold store, may sit briefly outside its calibration.

Discrimination trap

Latent heat is absorbed at constant temperature. It is the energy of the phase transition itself. Specific heat is the energy that produces a change in temperature within a single phase. Questions mix these deliberately, and the giveaway is whether the temperature is stated to be changing.

Why does the desflurane vaporiser not need a bimetallic strip?

Because it does not rely on ambient thermal equilibrium at all. It actively heats the agent to 39 °C and holds it under pressure, so its vapour pressure is a controlled constant rather than something drifting with the room and with use. It solves the same problem by electrical brute force rather than mechanical compensation.

Why do you feel cold stepping out of a pool, even into warm air?

Water evaporating from your skin draws its latent heat of vaporisation from you. The identical mechanism cools a vaporiser sump, and it is why alcohol on skin feels colder than water despite starting at the same temperature, since it evaporates faster.
