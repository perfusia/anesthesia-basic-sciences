Module 2 Exam

All seven sections plus the lab. Weighted toward partition coefficients, because Module 9 is built on them.

Exam conditions
  • First answer locks. No exceptions.
  • Separate the two coefficient questions before answering: speed is blood:gas, potency is oil:gas.
  • Watch for the SVP versus boiling point distinction on any altitude question.
  • Copy your misses at the end and paste them into chat.
Before you start

First answer is final. Change an answer only if you can name a concrete error: misread the stem, clicked the wrong option. Never because of a feeling.

The two traps this module is built to catch: low blood:gas means fast, not slow. And SVP is unaffected by altitude while boiling point falls.

Q1
Saturated vapour pressure varies with:
MechanismSVP is the equilibrium between escape and recapture at a liquid surface, set by the kinetic energy distribution. Only temperature and the identity of the substance affect it.
Q2
An agent with a blood:gas coefficient of 2.4 will show:
MechanismHigh solubility means blood absorbs agent avidly, holding alveolar partial pressure down until the reservoir approaches saturation. The same logic reversed makes washout slow.
Q3
The oil:gas partition coefficient predicts:
MechanismVia the Meyer-Overton correlation, potency tracks lipid solubility, and potency is expressed as MAC. Higher oil:gas gives lower MAC.
Q4
Ascending to altitude, a volatile agent's saturated vapour pressure:
MechanismSVP depends only on temperature. Boiling point is what changes at altitude, because the lower ambient pressure now meets the unchanged SVP curve at a lower temperature.
Q5
Desflurane's saturated vapour concentration at 20 °C and one atmosphere is:
Mechanism669 / 760 = 88%, far above any other agent, which is why it cannot be handled by splitting-ratio dilution.
Q6
Gas solubility in blood as temperature rises:
MechanismWarming gives dissolved molecules the kinetic energy to leave the solvent, shifting equilibrium into the gas phase. This is the reverse of most solids.
Q7
The Ostwald coefficient is pressure-independent because dissolved and gaseous volumes:
MechanismHenry's law doubles dissolved molecules when pressure doubles, and Boyle's law halves the compared gas volume. The two cancel, preserving the ratio.
Q8
Carbon dioxide diffuses about 20 times faster than oxygen across the alveolar membrane because:
MechanismSolubility sits unmodified in Fick's numerator. CO2's higher molecular weight costs only about 15% because that term is under a square root.
Q9
Graham's law describes gas movement through an orifice and depends on:
MechanismWith no membrane to dissolve into, solubility is irrelevant and rate varies inversely with the square root of molecular weight.
Q10
Nitrous oxide expands an air-filled space because relative to nitrogen it is:
MechanismBlood delivers nitrous into the space far faster than it removes nitrogen, so net molecules accumulate. Compliant spaces expand and non-compliant ones develop pressure.
Q11
The bimetallic strip in a variable bypass vaporiser compensates for:
MechanismVaporisation draws latent heat and cools the liquid, lowering SVP and output. The strip bends and opens the vaporising chamber further to hold concentration constant.
Q12
Dissolved oxygen in plasma at a PaO2 of 100 mmHg is approximately:
Mechanism0.003 mL/dL/mmHg × 100 = 0.3 mL/dL, against roughly 20 mL/dL on haemoglobin. Only the dissolved fraction obeys Henry's law.
Q13
A liquid boils when saturated vapour pressure equals:
MechanismBelow that temperature, ambient pressure collapses bubbles forming within the bulk liquid. Because ambient pressure is the reference, boiling point falls at altitude.
Q14
Which agent combination is correctly described as fast in onset and weak in potency?
MechanismBlood:gas of 0.47 makes it fast, and oil:gas of 1.4 makes it very weak, giving a MAC of 104%. Speed and potency are governed by different coefficients.
Q15
Latent heat of vaporisation is absorbed:
MechanismThe energy breaks intermolecular bonds rather than raising kinetic energy, producing the plateau on a heating curve. Specific heat is the quantity that changes temperature.
Q16
Rewarming blood after hypothermic cardiopulmonary bypass risks:
MechanismCold blood holds more dissolved gas. Warming lowers solubility and the excess comes out of solution as bubbles, which is why rewarming is gradual and filters are used.
Q17
An agent with a high oil:gas coefficient has a low MAC.
MechanismHigher lipid solubility means greater potency, and potency is inversely expressed as MAC. Halothane at oil:gas 224 has a MAC of 0.75%.
Q18
Saturated vapour pressure is higher in a container holding more liquid agent.
MechanismSVP is an equilibrium property independent of quantity. A nearly empty sump produces the same vapour pressure as a full one at the same temperature.
Q19
Fick's law includes a solubility term but Graham's law does not.
MechanismFick describes diffusion through a membrane, which requires dissolving into it. Graham describes effusion through an orifice, where nothing dissolves.
Q20
Blood:gas partition coefficient of desflurane.
MechanismAbout 0.42, the lowest of the volatile agents, which is why its onset and offset are the fastest. Nitrous oxide is close behind at 0.47.
Q21
Approximate value of the product oil:gas × MAC across the volatile agents.
MechanismRoughly 150, ranging from about 94 to 168 across real agents. This is the Meyer-Overton correlation expressed as a usable estimate.
Q22
Name the two properties predicted by blood:gas and oil:gas coefficients, in that order.
MechanismBlood:gas governs how fast alveolar partial pressure rises, so it predicts speed. Oil:gas governs affinity for the lipid target, so it predicts potency and therefore MAC.
