Module 7 Exam

All nine sections plus the lab. Weighted toward the parameter relationships, because Module 8 assumes them.

Exam conditions
  • First answer locks. No exceptions.
  • For any dosing question, decide first whether it is a volume question or a clearance question.
  • Half-life changes are ambiguous until you know which parameter moved.
  • Copy your misses at the end and paste them into chat.
Before you start

First answer is final. Change an answer only if you can name a concrete error: misread the stem, clicked the wrong option. Never because of a feeling.

The three traps in this module: loading dose depends on Vd alone while maintenance depends on Cl alone; a prolonged half-life does not identify which parameter changed; and reduced protein binding does not produce a sustained rise in free concentration at steady state.

First answer locks · mechanism appears on lock
Q1
Volume of distribution and clearance are best described as:
MechanismVd describes distribution, Cl describes removal, and they vary independently. t½ = 0.693 Vd/Cl combines them, which is why it cannot identify which one changed.
Q2
Loading dose is calculated as:
MechanismA loading dose fills a volume, so only Vd appears. Cl × target concentration gives the maintenance rate, which replaces what is eliminated.
Q3
A drug with a volume of distribution of 500 L in a 70 kg adult indicates:
MechanismVd is dose over plasma concentration. Extensive tissue binding drives plasma concentration very low and inflates the apparent volume far beyond any real body space.
Q4
Clearance of fentanyl is most reduced by:
MechanismFentanyl is high-extraction and flow-limited, so delivery rather than enzyme capacity is rate-limiting. Enzyme changes have little effect.
Q5
Which drug's clearance is most sensitive to enzyme inhibition?
MechanismPhenytoin is low-extraction and capacity-limited, so enzyme activity is the bottleneck. The other three are high-extraction and depend on hepatic blood flow.
Q6
Zero-order kinetics implies that:
MechanismThe eliminating process is saturated and works at maximum rate independent of concentration. Half-life is undefined because time to halve depends on the starting value.
Q7
First-order and zero-order kinetics are:
MechanismBelow Km the Michaelis-Menten expression reduces to first-order proportionality; above Km it reduces to the constant Vmax. Any drug can show either, depending on concentration.
Q8
The elimination rate constant k equals:
MechanismDividing elimination rate Cl·C by amount present Vd·C cancels concentration and leaves Cl/Vd. That cancellation is why first-order systems have a constant rate constant.
Q9
Half-life is correctly given by:
MechanismFrom t½ = ln2/k with k = Cl/Vd. Half-life rises with volume and falls with clearance.
Q10
Two drugs have identical half-lives but drug A has ten times the clearance. Drug A also has:
Mechanismt½ = 0.693 Vd/Cl. For half-life to be equal with tenfold clearance, volume of distribution must also be tenfold greater.
Q11
Approximately what proportion of steady state is reached after three half-lives?
MechanismEach half-life closes half the remaining gap: 50, 75, 87.5, 93.8, 96.9. Filling up and washing out are the same exponential in opposite directions.
Q12
Doubling an infusion rate has what effect on time to steady state?
MechanismTime to steady state is set by the elimination rate constant alone. A higher rate raises the plateau on an identical schedule; only a loading dose accelerates arrival.
Q13
Oral bioavailability of morphine is approximately:
MechanismMorphine is high-extraction, so most of the absorbed dose is removed on first pass through the liver. Oral doses are roughly three times intravenous doses.
Q14
Area under the concentration-time curve depends on:
MechanismAUC = F × Dose/Cl. Volume of distribution alters the shape of the curve but not the area, since concentration and duration effects cancel in the integral.
Q15
Which route reliably avoids first-pass hepatic metabolism?
MechanismSublingual venous drainage enters the superior vena cava directly. Oral, nasogastric, and upper rectal absorption all drain into the portal circulation.
Q16
Acidic drugs such as warfarin bind principally to:
MechanismAlbumin binds acidic drugs and α₁-acid glycoprotein binds basic ones. The two are negative and positive acute phase reactants respectively.
Q17
In hypoalbuminaemia, a total phenytoin level of 6 mg/L most likely represents:
MechanismReduced binding lowers total concentration while compensatory clearance restores free concentration near baseline. Increasing the dose on the total value is a recognised route to toxicity.
Q18
Reduced α₁-acid glycoprotein in pregnancy raises the free fraction of:
MechanismBupivacaine is a weak base bound by α₁-acid glycoprotein. The other three are acidic drugs bound principally by albumin.
Q19
Lidocaine at pH 7.4 with a pKa of 7.9 is approximately what percentage unionised?
Mechanism100/(1 + 10^(7.9 − 7.4)) = 100/4.16 = 24%. In infected tissue at pH 6.4 this falls to about 3%, which is why blocks fail there.
Q20
Ion trapping of local anaesthetic in a distressed fetus occurs because:
MechanismLocal anaesthetics are weak bases. They cross unionised, ionise in the more acidic fetal circulation, and cannot cross back, so they accumulate.
Q21
A prolonged half-life may result from an expanded volume of distribution with entirely normal clearance.
Mechanismt½ = 0.693 Vd/Cl, so raising Vd prolongs half-life without any impairment of elimination. Fluid resuscitation does exactly this to hydrophilic drugs.
Q22
Clearance is constant in first-order kinetics while the rate of elimination falls with concentration.
MechanismRate = Cl × C. With clearance fixed, the absolute rate scales with concentration. Conflating the two makes zero-order kinetics impossible to reason about.
Q23
Give the equation for maintenance infusion rate.
MechanismMaintenance rate = Cl × target concentration. It replaces exactly what is being eliminated, which is why volume of distribution does not appear.
Q24
Name the parameter that determines loading dose and the one that determines maintenance dose, in that order.
MechanismLoading dose fills a volume so depends on Vd; maintenance replaces elimination so depends on Cl. This is why renal failure changes the maintenance dose alone.
